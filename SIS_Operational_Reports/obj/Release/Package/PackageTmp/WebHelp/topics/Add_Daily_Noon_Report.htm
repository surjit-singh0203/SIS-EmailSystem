Add Daily Noon Report

Prepare Daily Noon Report

When you click on “Add Noon Report” button, it opens a form where the following fields are to be filled for adding a new report ----

 

a)       Voyage Number: A Voyage Number is a unique number used to identify a voyage. This is used for informational purposes. The Vessel ID identifies an actual equipment or ship that is used in the voyage schedule.

b)      At Sea/In Port: This refers to the vessel’s location/position whether it is in Sea or Port.

c)       Leg: A voyage leg is defined as a voyage between ports.

d)      Status (Laden/Ballast): This refers to the Vessel status/condition whether it is loaded condition or Ballast Condition.

e)      In Port Status: It refers to the status of the port whether the vessel is loading, discharging, etc. in port.

f)        Report Date: It refers to the date on which the Noon Report the date is prepared.

g)       Draft Fwd. (Mtrs): The draft forward (bow) is a draft measured in Meters at the perpendicular of the bow.

h)      Draft Mid (Mtrs): The draft mid is a draft measured in Meters at the midship.

i)        Draft Aft (Mtrs): The draft aft (stern) is draft measured in Meters at the perpendicular of the stern. 

j)        Latitude: Latitude is a measurement on a globe or map of location north or south of the Equator.

k)       Longitude: Longitude is a measurement of location east or west of the prime meridian at Greenwich, London, England. Longitude is measured 180° both east and west of the prime meridian.

l)        Displacement (MT): Displacement is the weight of water that a ship pushes aside when it is floating, which in turn is the weight of a ship (and its contents). It is usually measured when the ship's fuel tanks are full, and all stores are aboard.

m)    ETA (Date selection): It refers to the “Estimated Time of Arrival” of a vessel to your arrival point.

n)   CP Speed (Kts): Also known as  “Charter Party Speed”, refers to the pre-defined Speed of a vessel provided by the Charter Party in knots.

 

Navigation

 

·         Speed – Distance – Time

a)       Dist Noon to Noon (DMG)(NM): This distance refers to the distance covered by a ship from Noon to Noon.

b)      Log Dist (NM): This refers to the distance measured with respect to the surface reflecting the transmitted wave.

c)       Engine Dist(NM): Engine distance refers to the distance made by the engine via calculating the total revolutions of the propeller. The engine distance is calculated using the pitch of the propeller provided by the manufacturer.

d)      Total Distance (Dep to Cur) (NM): Total distance refers to the distance covered by a ship from Departure to the current position.

e)      Dist to Go (Nm): Distance to go refers to the actual distance remaining for the next port.

f)        Stmg Time Noon to Noon (Hrs) (Max 26Hrs): Steaming time refers to the actual time taken to cover the distance from Noon to Noon.

g)       Total Time (Dep to Cur) (Hrs): Total time refers to the actual time taken to cover the distance from Departure to current position.

h)      Actual Speed Noon to Noon (Kts): Actual speed refers to the speed taken to cover the distance from Noon to Noon.

i)        Gen Avg Speed (Dep to Cur) (Kts): Average speed refers to the actual speed taken to cover the distance from Departure to current position.

·         Weather

a)       Sea State: The degree of turbulence at sea, generally measured on a scale of 0 to 9 according to average wave height.

b)      Wind Direction: The direction from which the wind blows.

c)       Wind Force (BF Scale): The total force exerted by the wind on a structure or part of a structure.

d)      Swell Direction: This is the direction that the swells are coming from

e)      Swell Height (mtrs): It refers to the average size of the swell out at sea.

f)        Wave length (mtrs): It refers to the distance between two identical points on successive waves

g)       Wave Height (mtrs): It refers to the vertical distance between the crest (peak) and the trough of a wave.

·         Non – Routine Events

a)       Stoppage at Sea (Owners/Charterers Account and Hours): Ship is considered stopped when she is stopped w.r.t water in which she is floating.

b)      Deviation (Owners/Charterers Account and Hours): A deviation is a departure from the "agreed route" or the "usual route".

c)       Slow Steaming (Owners/Charterers Account and Hours): Slow steaming is a process of deliberately reducing the speed of a ship to cut down fuel consumption and carbon emissions.

d)      Bad Weather (Owners/Charterers Account and Hours): Bad weather can generally be defined as seas, swell, and wind conditions combining to exceed 8 feet and/or winds exceeding 30 knots.

e)      COT Preparation (Owners/Charterers Account and Hours): During loading/discharging oil cargo in a tanker, vessel is required to prepare cargo oil tanks for safe operation.

f)        Cargo Heating (Owners/Charterers Account and Hours): It refers to the time taken for the cargo oil in a tank to heat.

g)       BW Exchange (Owners/Charterers Account and Hours): Ballast Water Exchange (BWE) is a process involving the replacement of water in a ballast tank using either the sequential, flow-through, dilution or other exchange methodologies recommended or required by IMO.

·      Noon Report Remarks.: It refers to any additional activity/notice/comment/observation/declaration etc. to be added in the report.

 

Engine

·         Engine

a)       SLIP: The difference between the actual distance travelled by a ship and the theoretical distance given by the product of the propeller pitch and the number of revolutions. It is usually expressed as a percentage.

Slip (percent) = Slip (percent) = [(Engine distance – ship's distance)/ Engine's distance] X 100

b)      RPM: Revolutions per Minute is the number of full turns or rotations of a propeller in one minute.

c)       BHP(HP): Brake horsepower is the measurements of the power of the engine at the flywheel or crankshaft after the engine losing power due to the drive train and the resistance of the gearbox. Horsepower (hp) is the unit of measurement of the power or the rate at which the work is performed.

d)      MCR: Maximum Continuous Rating is the maximum power output engine can produce while running continuously at safe limits and conditions.

e)      M/E Control Location: It refers to the location of main engine controls in a vessel.

f)        SCAV. Manifold Pressure (Bars): It refers to the pressure in Bars of Scavenge Air Manifold.

g)       SCAV. Temp (Deg Centigrade): It refers to the temperature in Deg Centigrade of Scavenge Air.

h)      Max Exhaust Temp (Deg Centigrade): It refers to the maximum value of main engine exhaust gas temperature.

i)        Min. Exhaust Temp (Deg Centigrade): It refers to the minimum value of main engine exhaust gas temperature.

j)        SW Temp (Deg Centigrade): It refers to the temperature of Sea Water.

k)       ER Temp (Deg Centigrade): It refers to the temperature in Engine Room.

·         Aux. Engine

a)       Running Hrs.: It refers to the no. of hours an auxiliary engine runs in a day.

b)      Load (KW): It refers to the power in KW being consumed on a vessel via auxiliary engine.

c)       Extra Run Reason: It refers for a comment/declaration etc. to be added in the report for running an auxiliary engine.

·         LO & HO Consumption

a)       MECC (Ltrs) (Consumption and ROB): It refers to Main Engine Crank Case Oil Tank where crank case oil of a Main Engine is stored in large volumes.

b)      MECYL (Ltrs) (Consumption and ROB): It refers to Main Engine Cylinder Oil Tank where Cylinder oil of a Main Engine is stored in large volumes.

c)       AECC (Ltrs) (Consumption and ROB): It refers to Auxiliary Engine Crank Case Oil Tank where crank case oil of an Auxiliary Engine is stored in large volumes.

d)      Hydraulic Oil (Ltrs) (Consumption and ROB): It refers to Hydraulic Oil Tank where Hydraulic oil of the ship is stored in large volumes.

·         Fuel ROB in MT: Fuel remaining on board, refers to the quantity of fuel left on the vessel at the end of each month, or the end of a voyage.

a)       VLSFO (EOSP and FWE): Very Low Sulphur Fuel Oil (VLSFO) or Low Sulphur Fuel Oil (LSFO) is a type of marine fuel which contains less Sulphur and therefore complies with the IMO 2020 regulations.

b)      MDO (EOSP and FWE): Marine Diesel Oil (MDO) is a type of fuel oil and is a blend of gasoil and heavy fuel oil, with more gasoil than intermediate fuel oil used in the maritime field. Marine Diesel Oil is also called "Distillate Marine Diesel"

·         Other ROB

a)       Oxygen (Bottles): It refers to the oxygen bottles used for the standard maintenance equipment on ships for welding, burning, and heating.

b)      Acetylene (Bottles): It refers to the acetylene bottles used for the standard maintenance equipment on ships for welding, burning, and heating.

·         E/R Tanks

a)       Bilge: Bilge tank refers to a tank used for collecting any leakages in engine room such as oil, water, etc.

b)      Sludge: Sludge tank refers to a tank used for collecting sludge generated after treating fuel or lube. oil through purifiers.

c)       Waste Oil: Waste Oil tank refers to a tank used for collecting/evaporating sludge oil and preparing it for Incineration.

·         Fuel Consumption in MT

a)       VLSFO:

i)        Main Engine ACT: It refers to the fuel (VLSFO/MDO) consumed “AT SEA/MANOEUV        /ANCHOR/WAIT/BERTH” in main engine.

ii)       Aux Engine ACT: It refers to the fuel (VLSFO/MDO) consumed “AT SEA/MANOEUV        /ANCHOR/WAIT/BERTH” in Auxiliary engine.

iii)     Boiler ACT: It refers to the fuel (VLSFO/MDO) consumed “AT SEA/MANOEUV /ANCHOR/WAIT/BERTH” in Boiler.

iv)     Framo System ACT: It refers to the fuel (VLSFO/MDO) consumed “AT SEA/MANOEUV        /ANCHOR/WAIT/BERTH” in Framo System.

v)       IGG: It refers to the fuel (VLSFO/MDO) consumed in IGG System.

vi)     Events

(1)    Stoppage at Sea: It refers to the fuel (VLSFO/MDO) consumed during Stoppage at Sea.

(2)    Deviation: It refers to the fuel (VLSFO/MDO) consumed during Deviation of the vessel.

(3)    Slow Steaming: It refers to the fuel (VLSFO/MDO) consumed during Slow Steaming.

(4)    Bad Weather: It refers to the fuel (VLSFO/MDO) consumed during Bad Weather.

(5)    COT Prep: It refers to the fuel (VLSFO/MDO) consumed during Cargo Oil Tank Preparation.

(6)    Cargo Heating: It refers to the fuel (VLSFO/MDO) consumed during Cargo heating.

(7)    BW Exchange: It refers to the fuel (VLSFO/MDO) consumed during Ballast Water Exchange

(8)    Others: It refers to the fuel (VLSFO/MDO) consumed other than those listed.

      Cargo

     

·         Fresh Water

i)        FW Generated (MT): It refers to the amount of Fresh Water generated from Noon to report during the voyage

ii)       Consumption (MT): It refers to the amount of Fresh Water consumed from Noon to report during the voyage

iii)     ROB (MT): It refers to the amount of Fresh Water Remaining from Noon to report during the voyage

·         Slops ROB

i)        Oil(M3): It refers to the oily water residues accumulating in the slop tanks, including tank washing waters, because of washing of the cargo tanks in ships.

ii)       Water (m3): It refers to the water accumulated in the slop tanks.

·         Ballast

i)        ROB: It refers to the ballast water remaining on board a Vessel.

·         Other Soundings in mtrs

i)        Pump Room bilge max sounding: It refers to sounding measured in bilges of a pump room such as oil, water, etc.

ii)       Chain Locker 1: It refers to the sounding measured in Chain Locker space.

iii)     Chain Locker 2: It refers to the sounding measured in Chain Locker space.

·         Cargo Tanks

i)        Tank: It refers to the name of the Cargo Oil Tank.

ii)       Ullage (mtrs): This refers to the measurement of space between the surface of liquid in a tank and the top of the tank inner surface.

iii)     MT Qty: It refers to the quantity of Cargo in a tank.

iv)     Oxygen (% Volume): It refers to the percentage volume of oxygen gas content present in a tank.

v)       H2S (PPM): It refers to parts per million of Hydrogen sulfide gas present in a tank.

vi)     HC (% Volume): It refers to the percentage volume of Hydrocarbon gas present in a tank.

·          Ballast Tanks

i)        Sounding (mtrs): It refers to the sounding measured in ballast tanks.

ii)       Cubic Vol: It refers to the Volume occupied by a liquid in a tank.

iii)   HC (% Volume): It refers to the percentage volume of Hydrocarbon gas present in a ballast tank.
