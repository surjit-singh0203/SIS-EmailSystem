Prepare Departure Report

When you click on “Add Departure Report” button, it opens a form where the following fields are to be filled for adding a new report ----

a)       Voyage Number: A Voyage Number is a unique number used to identify a voyage. This is used for informational purposes. The Vessel ID identifies an actual equipment or ship that is used in the voyage schedule.

b)      Leg: A voyage leg is defined as a voyage between ports.

c)       Dep. Port: It refers to a port where vessel departs/casts off its last line.

d)      Next Leg: It refers to a starting point and ending point of a vessel’s voyage.

e)      Next Port: It refers to a port/consecutive place where a ship will arrive after having made a voyage.

f)        ETA: Estimated time of arrival refers to the estimated time around which a vessel will arrive at its next position/location.

g)       Report Date: It refers to a date on which the Report is prepared.

h)      Draft Fwd. (Mtrs): The draft forward (bow) is a draft measured in Meters at the perpendicular of the bow.

i)        Draft Mid (Mtrs): The draft mid is a draft measured in Meters at the midship.

j)        Draft Aft (Mtrs): The draft aft (stern) is draft measured in Meters at the perpendicular of the stern. 

Navigation

·         Maneuvering: Manoeuvring is an operation during which a vessel enters or exits coastal waters of a country, crosses several ships on the way, and proceeds towards or departs from a berth or jetty of a port. A ship may need to maneuver not only while arriving or departing a port but also while crossing canals and traffic zones.

a)       Maneuvering Hours: It refers to the number of hours consumed for maneuvering.

b)      Maneuvering Distance: It refers to the distance covered during maneuvering.

·         Weather

a)       Sea State: The degree of turbulence at sea, generally measured on a scale of 0 to 9 according to average wave height.

b)      Wind Direction: The direction from which the wind blows.

c)       Wind Force (BF Scale): The total force exerted by the wind on a structure or part of a structure.

d)      Swell Direction: This is the direction that the swells are coming from

e)      Swell Height (mtrs): It refers to the average size of the swell out at sea.

f)        Wave length (mtrs): It refers to the distance between two identical points on successive waves

g)       Wave Height (mtrs): It refers to the vertical distance between the crest (peak) and the trough of a wave.

·         Non – Routine Events

a)       Stoppage at Sea (Owners/Charterers Account and Hours): Ship is considered stopped when she is stopped w.r.t water in which she is floating.

b)      Deviation (Owners/Charterers Account and Hours): A deviation is a departure from the "agreed route" or the "usual route".

c)       Slow Steaming (Owners/Charterers Account and Hours): Slow steaming is a process of deliberately reducing the speed of a ship to cut down fuel consumption and carbon emissions.

d)      Bad Weather (Owners/Charterers Account and Hours): Bad weather can generally be defined as seas, swell, and wind conditions combining to exceed 8 feet and/or winds exceeding 30 knots.

e)      COT Preparation (Owners/Charterers Account and Hours): During loading/discharging oil cargo in a tanker, vessel is required to prepare cargo oil tanks for safe operation.

f)        Cargo Heating (Owners/Charterers Account and Hours): It refers to the time taken for the cargo oil in a tank to heat.

g)       BW Exchange (Owners/Charterers Account and Hours): Ballast Water Exchange (BWE) is a process involving the replacement of water in a ballast tank using either the sequential, flow-through, dilution or other exchange methodologies recommended or required by IMO.

·         Other Receipts, Repairs, Crew Change & Landed

a)       CTM (Mention Currency & Amount): Cash to Master refers to the cash received in the port.

b)      SPARES (Mention Revision Numbers): It refers to the receipts of spares received by vessel.

c)       STORES (Mention Revision Numbers): It refers to the receipts of stores received by vessel.

d)      REPAIRS CONDUCTED (Mention details): It refers to the details of repairs conducted by the vessel.            

e)      CREW CHANGE (No of Crew): It refers to details of crew change on the vessel.    

f)        ITEMS LANDED (Mention Details): It refers to the details of items landed ashore by the vessel.

·         Departure Report Remarks.: It refers to any additional activity/notice/comment/observation/declaration etc. to be added in the report.

Engine

·         Slops / Bilge Disposed & ROB

a)       Slops Disposed(m3): It refers to the amount of slops landed ashore/disposed by a vessel.              

b)      Slops ROB(m3): It refers to the amount of slops remaining on board a vessel after departure.

c)       Bilge Disposed(m3): It refers to the amount of bilge landed ashore/disposed by a vessel.

d)      Bilge ROB(m3): It refers to the amount of bilge remaining on board a vessel after departure.

·         Other Disposal

a)       Sludge(m3): It refers to the amount of sludge landed ashore/disposed by a vessel.            

b)      Garbage - Plastic(m3): It refers to the amount of plastic garbage landed ashore/disposed by a vessel.

c)       Garbage - Others(m3): It refers to the amount of other garbage landed ashore/disposed by a vessel.        

d)      Other Disposal(m3): It refers to the amount of any other disposals (apart from the above mentioned) landed ashore/disposed by a vessel.

·         Time & Position

a)       SBE: Standby Engines (time & position) refers to the time when the main engine is on standby and ready for maneuvering.

b)      RFA: Revolutions Full Away refers to the time and position when the vessel has reached its navigational speed for its next voyage.

·         Fuel ROB in MT: Fuel remaining on board, refers to the quantity of fuel left on the vessel at the end of each month, or the end of a voyage.

a)       VLSFO (EOSP and FWE): Very Low Sulphur Fuel Oil (VLSFO) or Low Sulphur Fuel Oil (LSFO) is a type of marine fuel which contains less Sulphur and therefore complies with the IMO 2020 regulations.

b)      MDO (EOSP and FWE): Marine Diesel Oil (MDO) is a type of fuel oil and is a blend of gasoil and heavy fuel oil, with more gasoil than intermediate fuel oil used in the maritime field. Marine Diesel Oil is also called "Distillate Marine Diesel"

·         Engine

a)       SLIP: The difference between the actual distance travelled by a ship and the theoretical distance given by the product of the propeller pitch and the number of revolutions. It is usually expressed as a percentage.

Slip (percent) = Slip (percent) = [(Engine distance – ship's distance)/ Engine's distance] X 100

b)      RPM: Revolutions per Minute is the number of full turns or rotations of a propeller in one minute.

c)       BHP(HP):Brake horsepower is the measurements of the power of the engine at the flywheel or crankshaft after the engine losing power due to the drive train and the resistance of the gearbox. Horsepower (hp) is the unit of measurement of the power or the rate at which the work is performed.

d)      MCR: Maximum Continuous Rating is the maximum power output engine can produce while running continuously at safe limits and conditions.

·         LO & HO Consumption

a)       MECC (Ltrs) (Consumption and ROB): It refers to Main Engine Crank Case Oil Tank where crank case oil of a Main Engine is stored in large volumes.

b)      MECYL (Ltrs) (Consumption and ROB): It refers to Main Engine Cylinder Oil Tank where Cylinder oil of a Main Engine is stored in large volumes.

c)       AECC (Ltrs) (Consumption and ROB): It refers to Auxiliary Engine Crank Case Oil Tank where crank case oil of an Auxiliary Engine is stored in large volumes.

d)      Hydraulic Oil (Ltrs) (Consumption and ROB): It refers to Hydraulic Oil Tank where Hydraulic oil of the ship is stored in large volumes.

·         Other ROB

a)       Oxygen (Bottles): It refers to the oxygen bottles used for the standard maintenance equipment on ships for welding, burning, and heating.

b)      Acetylene (Bottles): It refers to the acetylene bottles used for the standard maintenance equipment on ships for welding, burning, and heating.

·         Bunker Received in MT: Bunkering is the process of receiving oil in the ship's tank. Bunker received refers to the amount of bunker received by a vessel in Metric Tons.

·         Fuel Consumption in MT

a)       VLSFO:

i)        Main Engine ACT: It refers to the fuel (VLSFO/MDO) consumed “AT SEA/MANOEUV        /ANCHOR/WAIT/BERTH”in main engine.

ii)       Aux Engine ACT: It refers to the fuel (VLSFO/MDO) consumed “AT SEA/MANOEUV        /ANCHOR/WAIT/BERTH”in Auxiliary engine.

iii)     Boiler ACT: It refers to the fuel (VLSFO/MDO) consumed “AT SEA/MANOEUV /ANCHOR/WAIT/BERTH”in Boiler.

iv)     Framo System ACT: It refers to the fuel (VLSFO/MDO) consumed “AT SEA/MANOEUV        /ANCHOR/WAIT/BERTH”in Framo System.

v)       IGG: It refers to the fuel (VLSFO/MDO) consumed in IGG System.

vi)     Events

(1)    Stoppage at Sea: It refers to the fuel (VLSFO/MDO) consumed during Stoppage at Sea.

(2)    Deviation: It refers to the fuel (VLSFO/MDO) consumed during Deviation of the vessel.

(3)    Slow Steaming: It refers to the fuel (VLSFO/MDO) consumed during Slow Steaming.

(4)    Bad Weather: It refers to the fuel (VLSFO/MDO) consumed during Bad Weather.

(5)    COT Prep: It refers to the fuel (VLSFO/MDO) consumed during Cargo Oil Tank Preparation.

(6)    Cargo Heating: It refers to the fuel (VLSFO/MDO) consumed during Cargo heating.

(7)    BW Exchange: It refers to the fuel (VLSFO/MDO) consumed during Ballast Water Exchange

(8)    Others: It refers to the fuel (VLSFO/MDO) consumed other than those listed.
